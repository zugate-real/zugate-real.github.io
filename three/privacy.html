Three · Privacy

Your days stay on your phone.

Three has no account. Your habits, your days and anything you write stay on your phone. If you'd like to help make Three better, you can share short notes about how the app is used, like which screens you open and what you tap. That's off until you turn it on, and it never includes what your habits are called or anything you wrote.

At a glance

  • Stays on your phone

    Your habits and what they're called, your days, notes, photos, moods, reminder times, and anything you type into Help me choose. Your record isn't sent to the developer or to anyone else. If you choose to share how you use Three, only counts about it leave.

    What's kept, and where

  • Shared only if you'd like to help

    Which screens you open, what you tap, and roughly how your habits are going, by the day, under a random number instead of your name. It shows what's helping and what gets in the way. It goes to PostHog, is stored in the EU, and is deleted after a year.

    Off until you turn it on
    What's shared, and why

  • Handled by Apple

    Payments, refunds, Siri, and your phone's own backup. Apple's privacy policy covers those, and Three never sees your payment details.

    What Apple handles

  • Never

    No account. No ads. No tracking across other apps or websites. No selling. No location.

1. Who's responsible

In shortOne person makes Three and decides what happens to the usage records. Under data protection law, that person is the controller.

Who
Three's developer, one independent person
Email

2. What stays on your phone

In shortEverything you put into Three lives on your phone, in the app's own storage. Your record isn't sent to the developer or to anyone else. If you turn on sharing, counts about it do leave, such as that a habit was marked on a day, never which one (section 3).

  • Your habits, what each one comes after, and the days and times you marked them.
  • Notes, photos and moods from the journal. Photos are saved again when you add them, which drops their location and camera details.
  • Your answers to Three's questions, what you picked in Help me choose, your reminders and your settings.
  • What Three has noticed, such as when you usually do a habit. It's worked out on the phone.
  • A random number made on this phone. If you share how you use Three, the records are filed under it (section 3). Deleting the app removes it. Start over keeps it.
  • The date Three first opened on this phone, kept in the phone's Keychain so the free first day can't start again with a reinstall. It stays on this phone only and is never synced. The date itself is never sent, though shared records count days from it (section 3). iOS keeps it even after the app is deleted.

Widgets, the Lock Screen and Apple Watch

They read from the same storage. That means a habit's name can show on your Lock Screen, in a reminder, in the Live Activity and on your watch. Notes, moods and photos never do.

Your phone's backup

If your phone backs up to iCloud or to a computer, Three's data goes along with that backup. The backup is Apple's service, or your computer, not Three's.

3. Helping make Three better

In shortThree is made by one person, who can't sit beside you to see what helps and what gets in the way. If you'd like to help, turn on Settings → Share how you use Three. The app then sends short records of how it's used. They never include a habit's name, anything you wrote or the time you did something. It starts off, and you can turn it off whenever you like.

Why it helps

These records are how Three learns whether it's working: whether people reach a first sealed day and keep going, which parts help, which get in the way, and what breaks. The aim is patterns across everyone who shares, not what any one person does. They aren't used for ads, aren't sold, and aren't used to decide anything about you.

What a record says

Each record names one thing that happened, such as a screen opened, a habit marked, a day sealed or a question answered, with a few short labels and counts. It also carries:

  • the day it happened, with no time of day
  • how many days it's been since Three first opened on this phone
  • the app's version
  • the random number that stands for your phone
Every kind of record, listed
  • Opening the app, and finishing the first pages.
  • Screens and sheets opened, by a fixed name such as Today, All your days, the journal or Settings. Never what's on them.
  • That a short line was shown at the bottom of the screen, and which kind. Never the line.
  • Adding, changing, pausing, resuming, graduating or removing a habit, with counts such as how many days a week, and whether it came from Three's suggestions or your own words. Never its name.
  • Reaching the three-habit limit, or raising it.
  • Marking a habit, where from (the app, a widget, the Watch, a reminder), whether it was done before its reminder, and whether the day sealed.
  • How soon the first habit was marked after Three first opened, in rough bands.
  • Answers to Three's questions: why a day was missed (forgot, no time, didn't want to), whether a habit feels automatic, the one question about a habit you wrote yourself, and the extra question in a small experiment.
  • Which half of that experiment a habit is in. Half the habits that qualify are asked the extra question and half aren't, to see whether it helps.
  • What Three noticed or suggested, shown and answered, and how a two-week try of a change went. Only the kind, never the habit.
  • Tips shown, and how they were answered.
  • Reminders turned on or off, learned or fixed, how many are planned, a reminder opened, and whether notifications are allowed. Never their times.
  • Plan your day opened, and a part of the day saved.
  • A focus session started or ended, in minutes.
  • That a note was written, where, and roughly how long it was, in four sizes. Never a word of it.
  • That the Journal showed its line about your notes, and whether it was about hard days or good ones. Never the theme.
  • That a mood, a feeling or photos were saved, and how many photos. Never which mood or feeling.
  • Help me choose opened, whether something was typed, whether it found a match, and how many habits were saved. Never what was typed.
  • Which of Help me choose's own suggested habits were wanted or turned down, such as Pack a snack, and whether the typed line led to them. Only Three's list, never a habit you named or anything you wrote.
  • A habit said in one line, and whether Three understood it. Never the line.
  • The Live Activity started, and for how many habits.
  • The plans screen shown, a plan bought (Yearly or Lifetime) and purchases restored.
  • A month or the whole record shared as a picture, the record exported, moved to another phone, or a day put right.
  • Start over, with the reason picked from a short list.
  • Turning sharing on.
  • Whether the app's storage opened as it should.
  • Something that broke: where, and what kind of error. Never its message.

What a record never says

  • A habit's name
  • What it comes after
  • Notes or journal lines
  • What you type into Help me choose
  • Photos
  • Which mood or feeling
  • The time you did anything
  • Why you paused
  • Anything you write about health, food or weight
  • Your name or email
  • Your Apple Account
  • Contacts or location

Three has no analytics software from anyone else inside it, so nothing else can be picked up. No screen recording, no replays, no capture of what you type.

The random number

Records from one phone share the same random number. It isn't your name and isn't linked to your Apple Account, but it does tie one phone's records together. So they're pseudonymous rather than anonymous, and they're treated with the care personal data deserves.

Your consent

Sharing starts only when you turn the switch on, and that's the legal basis for it: your consent (GDPR Art. 6(1)(a)). You can take it back any time by turning the switch off.

Who receives them

PostHog, Inc., a US company, stores them for Three in its EU cloud in Frankfurt, Germany, under a data processing agreement. PostHog uses a few other companies to run its service. They're listed at posthog.com/subprocessors. Records travel over an encrypted connection.

Like any server, PostHog sees the phone's internet address when records arrive, and when they arrive. Records go in small batches, and when you leave the app, so the arrival time is roughly when Three was used. Three asks PostHog not to work out a place from the address, and PostHog is set to throw the address away.

Only the developer reads the records, from India. PostHog, Inc. relies on the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and standard contractual clauses.

How long they're kept

PostHog deletes each record a year after it arrives. On the phone, records wait in a short queue until they're sent in a batch.

Turning it off

Settings → Share how you use Three. Off stops sending at once and deletes anything still waiting on the phone, so nothing from before can be sent later. Everything in Three works the same either way.

4. Help me choose

In shortAnything you type into Help me choose is read on your phone and never sent.

Help me choose uses a small language model that comes inside the app and runs on the phone. What you type is kept in memory while Help me choose is open, so its cards can show what it understood, then cleared. It isn't saved and never leaves the phone.

If sharing is on, a record says only that Help me choose was used, whether you typed, whether it found a match, and which of its own suggestions you wanted. When a line is about a hard time, food or weight, no record says what it found.

The Journal uses the same model, on the phone, to see what a note is about, such as work or sleep, so it can say which days mention it. Notes and the themes found in them are never sent.

5. What Apple handles

In shortPayments, refunds, Siri and your phone's backup go through Apple, under Apple's privacy policy.

Purchases

Apple takes payment through the App Store. Three never sees your payment details, your name or your Apple Account. It learns only whether a plan is active. Restore works through your Apple Account, which is how a plan follows you to a new phone.

Siri and Shortcuts

When you ask Siri or run a shortcut, Apple handles the request, on the phone or on Apple's servers depending on your settings. Three receives only which habit to mark done, or that you asked how many days are sealed.

Reminders

Reminders are notifications the phone schedules itself. Nothing sends them from a server. Their words come from the words you typed.

6. Moving, exporting and sharing

In shortYour record leaves the phone only when you send it, and only to where you send it.

  • Export your days writes one file with every habit and every day.
  • Move to another phone makes one file locked with a code that's shown only on your phone. Nobody can open the file without the code, the developer included.
  • Sharing a month, a graduation or the whole record as an image sends that image where you choose.

7. Code from other people

In shortThree includes a few pieces of open-source code. None of them sends anything anywhere.

  • ONNX Runtime, from Microsoft, runs Help me choose's language model on the phone.
  • PopupView, from exyte, draws some of the app's sheets.
  • Phosphor Icons draws some of the app's icons.
  • The language model is built on Microsoft's multilingual E5.

There's no advertising, analytics, crash-reporting or social software from anyone else. Usage records are sent by the app's own code. Every licence is in the app, under Settings → Open-source licences.

8. Your rights and choices

In shortYou can stop sharing, take your record with you, and erase it, all from Settings. For usage records you also have legal rights.

Stop usage records
Turn off Settings → Share how you use Three.
Take your record
Settings → Export your days.
Erase your record
Settings → Start over. See Deleting your data.

In the EU, the UK and places with similar laws, you can ask to see, correct or delete personal data about you, to limit or object to its use, and to get a copy you can take elsewhere. You can take back your consent any time. That doesn't change what was done before.

To ask, write to three.support.indie@gmail.com. You'll get an answer within a month. Section 9 says honestly what can and can't be found.

9. Deleting your data

In shortWhat's on your phone, you can erase yourself, at once. Records already shared can't be picked out by anyone, so they're deleted after a year.

On your phone

Settings → Start over erases your habits, days, notes, photos and moods, your answers and what Three learned from them, straight away, with no copy left on this phone. Earlier backups of your phone keep theirs until they're replaced. What stays is what isn't your record: your settings, that you've seen the first pages, the random number and the Keychain date (section 2).

Usage records already sent

They carry no name, and nothing in the app shows you the number they're filed under. So today neither you nor the developer can pick out yours to delete them. Turning sharing off stops new ones at once and deletes any still waiting on the phone. What was already sent is deleted a year after it arrived.

10. Emails to support

In shortIf you write, your email is used only to answer you.

Emails are kept in Gmail, which Google runs. Only the developer reads them. They're kept only as long as it takes to answer you. The basis is answering what you asked (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)).

11. This website

These pages are hosted by GitHub Pages. GitHub logs each visitor's internet address for security, under GitHub's privacy statement. The support page has a few lines of its own script to search its answers. It runs in your browser and sends nothing.

12. Children

In shortThree asks no one for a name, an age or contact details.

Usage records stay off unless someone turns them on.

13. Where to complain

In shortWrite first. If you're not happy with the answer, you can complain to a data protection authority.

  • In the EU, the authority where you live or work. The European Data Protection Board lists them all.
  • In the UK, the Information Commissioner's Office.
