Three · Privacy Policy

Your days stay on your phone.

Three has no account and never sees your habits, your days or anything you wrote. The one thing that can leave your phone is a record of how the app is used — which screens are used, what is tapped, and whether the app is kept, by the day — and only if you turn it on in Settings. It starts off. The rest of this page says the same thing in the detail the App Store asks for.

What the app stores

Your habits, the days you marked them done, the answers you gave to its questions, and your settings. All of it is kept on your device, in the app's own storage, which its widgets can also read. None of it is sent to us or to anyone else.

What the app does not do

It does not create an account. It does not use advertising or tracking of any kind, and it never links anything to you.

Usage records

To learn whether the app works — whether people reach the first sealed day, keep going, or stop — Three can send a small record of its own use: the name of an event (a screen opened, a habit marked, a day sealed, a reminder opened, a question answered), counts and short labels such as “widget” or “yes”, the day it happened (never the time of day), the day since first launch, and the app's version. For a note, that means only that one was written, where, and roughly how long it was — never a word of it. For a habit Three has asked about, it means only rough words for what happened next, such as “steady” or “done less”. It never includes a habit's name, what it comes after, a note, a photo, a mood, the time you did something, or your name or Apple Account. Each phone is counted by an id drawn at random on this phone, with no link to you, your Apple Account or your device; because the records from one phone share that id, they are pseudonymous rather than anonymous. They go to PostHog, a processor hosted in the European Union, over an encrypted connection. Like any server, it sees the phone's internet address when they arrive; Three asks it not to work out a location from that address. They are not used for advertising. None of this is sent unless you turn on Settings → Share how you use Three, which starts off. Turning it off again stops sending at once and deletes anything still waiting on the phone.

Reminders

Reminders are local notifications, scheduled on your device. Their wording is built from the words you typed.

Purchases

Purchases are handled by Apple through the App Store. Three never sees your payment details. Your purchase is tied to your Apple Account, which is how Restore works on a new phone.

Siri and Shortcuts

If you use Siri or Shortcuts with Three, the request is handled by Apple on your device. The app only receives which habit to mark done.

Export and sharing

You can export your record as a file, or share a month as an image. That data leaves your phone only when you choose to send it somewhere, and only to where you send it.

Other companies’ code

Three contains no third-party SDKs — no advertising, attribution, crash-reporting or social libraries. The usage records described above are sent by a few dozen lines of the app’s own code over a normal encrypted connection, not by someone else’s software running inside the app.

Deleting your data

Settings → Start over erases everything the app holds on your phone, immediately and with no copy kept. There is no account to close. For usage records, see the section above: the switch stops them and clears what is waiting, and you can email us to have what was already sent removed.

Children

Three is rated 4+. It asks no one for a name, an age or contact details, and usage records stay off unless someone turns them on.

Changes

If any of this ever changes, this page will say so, and the app's version notes will say so too.
